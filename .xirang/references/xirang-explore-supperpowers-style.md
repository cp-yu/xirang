# Explore design guide

Output language: use the user's main language for prose and non-canonical section labels; keep commands, paths, artifact names, schema keys, and Xirang tokens unchanged.

## Design loop

Use a todo checklist when available: evidence → applicable decisions → confirmation → self-review → Summary review. Scale each stage to the actual decisions; a narrow change still needs a clear, confirmed problem, scope, approach and verification method. Reuse confirmations already supplied instead of asking for them again.

1. Understand the problem behind the user's input before settling an approach. The user may express an intent, a proposed approach, or both; do not assume the input is complete. Establish the current situation, desired outcome and constraints needed for this decision, distinguishing suggested approaches from explicit requirements. Ground existing-project claims in the Semantic Model, relevant Change, implementation, tests and Git evidence. Identify material unknowns and affected subsystem boundaries. For independent subsystems, clarify scope and recommend an implementation order.
2. Actively clarify gaps that could change the problem definition, choice of approach or judgment of success. Investigate facts available from project evidence; ask the user about unclear goals, preferences and trade-offs. When asking, explain your current understanding and the gap, ask one focused question at a time and wait. Reuse established context; continue when the relevant gaps are resolved rather than following a fixed questionnaire. Resolve terminology before dependent choices. Assess both user-proposed and Agent-proposed approaches against the intended outcome and constraints. Distinguish established facts, reasoning and assumptions that could affect the decision. Look for constraints or counterexamples that could invalidate an approach, including your own recommendation. Adopting an approach does not establish its technical feasibility. For a key choice, compare 2–3 viable approaches with trade-offs, fit and a reasoned recommendation; include a simpler alternative when useful. Do not manufacture alternatives for settled decisions. Use diagrams only when they clarify architecture, state, flow or dependencies; concise text or tables suffice for routine CLI or field decisions. Do not introduce a browser/server companion for these.
3. Confirm applicable mechanisms or design sections incrementally: behavior and acceptance outcomes, architecture/components, data flow, technology, testing and risks. Preserve conditions, numbers, exceptions, event order and timing. Actively surface consequential failure, boundary and competing-event choices instead of leaving them for Propose to invent. A failed approach does not by itself imply misunderstood intent or an unachievable goal. Reassess the approach and its assumptions, then recheck dependent decisions after any revision. Apply the Design Record protocol immediately to each confirmation.
4. Use available evidence and reasoning to assess feasibility; not every design needs a new test. When a consequential feasibility question cannot be resolved adequately this way, delegate the question, constraints and decision background to Experimenter before confirming the dependent design. It independently designs and runs a minimal discriminating experiment, choosing an appropriate location with the main Agent. Inspect reproducible observations and limitations. Use partial evidence for what it establishes without treating a local success as proof of the whole approach. If execution is blocked or results are inconclusive, state what remains unknown and how it affects the decision. Experimental success is evidence, not user adoption. Experiment files are not formal implementation.
5. For verification, use Test Quality Guidance: identify the behavior at risk and existing coverage, then choose reuse/modify/add/delete/one-time with reasons. Name the authoritative suite when several exist. Include obsolete-test handling when needed and a One-time Verification subsection for evidence-only checks. Complete Contracts, Acceptance Cases and tasks belong to Propose.

## Summary gate and handoff

Before a ready Summary:
- Resolve contradictions, vague boundaries, and target-affecting pending choices. User agreement is required to exclude or defer in-scope behavior. Correct source-determined expression or reasoning errors yourself; ask for new or changed decisions.
- Exercise relevant boundary/competing-event sequences against the design, and check necessary experimental evidence, testing strategy, risks and semantic impact coverage.
- Pass the shared Design Record **Record decision and Summary gate**. It owns complete latest-record reload, capture completeness, conflicts and filename handoff.

Present the Design Summary in the conversation only. Preserve confirmed scope, behavior, rationale, conditions, numbers, exceptions, timing and acceptance outcomes, with applicable architecture, technology, testing and risks. Distinguish confirmed decisions, recommendations, unresolved items and user-approved exclusions. When record-backed, identify its filename without copying the record. Do not create a separate decision ledger.

Stop after presenting the Summary for user review. User changes return to the affected design section and dependent checks. Only after the user accepts the Summary may they trigger xirang-propose; Explore does not author a complete Change, update formal model/generated work surfaces, commit or implement. Design Record operations and delegated experimental writes follow their own permissions.
